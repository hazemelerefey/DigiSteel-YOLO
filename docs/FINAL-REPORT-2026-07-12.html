3. Experiment Phases — Detailed Analysis

3.1 Phase 1: Initial Baseline & Failed Attempts (Weeks 1–3)

Experiment 01 — Old Baseline 75.8% mAP
Architecture: YOLOv11n + GhostConv + WFCA + EMA (6 custom modules). mosaic=1.0, imgsz=800.
Result: 75.8% mAP@0.5. Only +0.1% over vanilla YOLOv11n. Attention stacking failure — modules learned to ignore each other.
Experiment 06 — Week 3 A1: Config Fix 74.8% mAP
Change: mosaic=0, mixup=0.15, degrees=10, translate=0.2, scale=0.6, shear=5, epochs=400.
Result: 74.8% mAP@0.5 (−1.0%). Removing mosaic alone hurt augmentation diversity.
Experiment 07 — Week 3 A2: Architecture Fix 73.4% mAP
Change: Upgraded to YOLOv11s (2× capacity) + Standard Conv + CoordAttention at P3.
Result: 73.4% mAP@0.5 (−2.4%). YOLOv11s overfit severely on only 1,200 training images.
Key Lesson
Both naive config changes and architecture upsizing failed. A systematic, recipe-first approach was needed.

3.2 Phase 2: Recipe Optimization & DAFE (Week 4)

Experiment 08 — Fresh Baseline (The Breakthrough) 78.8% mAP
Recipe: AdamW, lr0=0.001, 600 epochs, patience=150, cos_lr=True, mosaic=0.0, mixup=0.15, copy_paste=0.1, imgsz=800, label_smoothing=0.01.
Result: 78.8% mAP@0.5, 45.2% mAP@0.5:0.95 (+3.0%). Training time: 2.57 hours.
Verdict: Biggest single improvement in the entire project. Proper recipe optimization matters more than architecture changes.
Experiment 10 — DAFE v4 (Novel Architecture) 80.3% mAP
Architecture: YOLOv11n + DAFE (Defect-Aware Feature Enhancement) at P2 and P3.
DAFE Design: Dual-branch — Sobel EdgeConv (linear defects) + local variance TextureBranch (surface anomalies) + SE channel attention + learnable residual (α_init=−2.2).
Result: 80.3% mAP@0.5 (+1.5%). Precision: 85.2% (+13.3%). FPS: 110. Crazing: +8.3%, Inclusion: +3.6%, Scratches: +1.3%.
Key Lesson
One well-designed module (DAFE = +1.5%) beats six stacked modules (GhostConv+WFCA+EMA = +0.1%).

3.3 Phase 3: Transfer Learning

Experiments 12–13 — Two-Stage Transfer Learning 79.4% mAP
Stage 1 (Head-only): 77.8% mAP@0.5 (−1.0%). Stage 2 (Fine-tune all): 79.4% mAP@0.5 (+0.6%).
Per-class gains: Inclusion +5.4%, Crazing +7.1%. Modest improvement — from-scratch with proper recipe is competitive.
Experiment 14a — TL + DAFE v1 (Critical Failure) 63.3% mAP
Result: 63.3% mAP@0.5 (−15.5% catastrophic). DAFE modules inserted into fine-tuned model disrupted learned features.
Recovery (14b): 78.6% mAP@0.5 (−0.2%). Managed to recover baseline but DAFE gave no additional benefit with TL.

3.4 Phase 4: YOLOv26n Investigation

Experiment 15 — YOLOv26n from Scratch 81.0% mAP
Architecture: YOLOv26n (~2.9M params). Same optimized recipe as fresh baseline.
Result: 81.0% mAP@0.5, 43.1% mAP@0.5:0.95. Best mAP@0.5. Crazing: 55.1% (best across all). Training: 3.19 hours.
Experiment 16 — YOLOv26n Transfer Learning 75.6% mAP
Stage 1: 74.4% (−4.4%). Stage 2: 75.6% (−3.2%). Transfer learning HURT YOLOv26n.
Why: COCO weights conflicted with steel domain. From-scratch training with proper recipe was superior.
Critical Insight
Transfer learning is overrated for this dataset. From-scratch training with proper recipe matched or beat TL in most configurations.

5.2 DAFE Architecture Detail

DAFE (Defect-Aware Feature Enhancement) is the core novelty of DigiSteel-YOLO:

Edge Branch

Conv2d(C, C//2, 3×3) initialized with Sobel-X/Y filters. All weights learnable. Targets linear defects (scratches, crazing).

Texture Branch

AvgPool(3×3) → local variance (E[X²]−E[X]²) → Conv1×1. Lightweight. Targets surface anomalies (inclusions, pitting).

Channel Attention

SE-style squeeze-excitation after concatenation of both branches. Learns which features matter most.

Learnable Residual

output = x + sigmoid(α_raw) × enhanced. α_init = −2.2 → sigmoid ≈ 0.1 (starts small, learns to contribute).

Placement Strategy
DAFE is placed at P2 (80×80 at imgsz=640, highest spatial resolution) and P3 (40×40). Not at P4/P5 — those lose fine-grained spatial info critical for defect detection.

8.3 Critical Insights

Recipe > Architecture
The optimized training recipe alone gave +3.0%. DAFE on top gave +1.5%. Always optimize the baseline first.
Crazing is the Bottleneck
At 48.6% (DAFE) to 55.1% (YOLOv26n), crazing remains the hardest class. Fine cracks at 200px are extremely challenging.
Precision-Recall Trade-off
DAFE improved precision (+13.3%) but reduced recall (−6.3%). More conservative but fewer false positives.
Transfer Learning is Overrated Here
From-scratch training with proper recipe matched or beat TL in most configurations. COCO pretraining hurts when the domain (steel) is far from natural images.
Robustness Matters
DAFE showed +10.4% on motion blur and +7.1% on JPEG compression — real-world relevant perturbations that matter for deployment.

9. Recommendations for Future Work

To Reach 83%+ mAP@0.5

  • Multi-dataset training: Combine NEU-DET with GC10-DET (3,570 additional grayscale steel images)
  • DAFE on YOLOv26n: Apply DAFE module to YOLOv26n backbone (not yet tested)
  • Advanced augmentation: AutoAugment, Mosaic9, or MixUp variants optimized for defect textures
  • Class-balanced sampling: Oversample crazing or use focal loss with class-specific gamma

To Improve Crazing Detection

  • Higher resolution crops: Train on 1024+ with crazing-focused cropping
  • Edge-specific pre-processing: Sobel/Canny as additional input channel
  • Synthetic data: Generate synthetic crazing patterns to augment 689 real samples

For Deployment

  • ONNX export with TensorRT optimization for real-time inference (>100 FPS)
  • Model pruning to reduce parameters while maintaining accuracy
  • Robustness hardening through perturbation-aware training
